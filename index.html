00 / The research journey

Build a piece.
Find its limits.
Build the next.

The starting idea was simple: estimate each horse’s chance of winning, eventually an hour before the race, and compare those probabilities with the market.

That idea quickly became several different problems. Which horses were actually declared? What information existed at the deadline? Can a language model extract a recommendation without inventing one? And does that recommendation add anything to a sensible statistical model?

This notebook follows those dependencies. The sequence is a didactic reconstruction of the work, rather than a verbatim session log. Recorded experiments retain their actual counts and limitations. Invented examples are marked Illustration.

Where we are now

A working local research pipeline and a small development evaluation. Historical input availability is not independently certified. The latest article experiment improves point scores, with important extraction, coverage and uncertainty limitations. A dependable forecasting gain has not been established.

01 / Recover the world

Recorded implementation

First, define
what a race is.

Before predicting a winner, we needed a stable record of the race, its field and its outcome.

The initial SQLite study grew from 12 official JCE races into a historical backfill from Masdehipódromos. The populated research database contains 1,593 selected races, 312 meetings and 12,872 runner-result rows across eight Spanish venues, from November 2022 to November 2026. This is collected coverage, not certified national completeness.

Snapshots preserve the acquired text, original URL, SHA-256 and observation time. Revisions remain separate. Provider IDs are retained; similarly spelled names do not automatically establish that two providers mean the same horse. Append-only tables provide an application audit trail.

The first useful mistake

Result pages can tell us who won. Using their fields as historical forecast inputs, however, can reveal who eventually ran and omit earlier declared horses. The first H1 2026 benchmark was therefore a retrospective diagnostic: 161 races and 1,395 result-derived runner records.

We rebuilt inputs from 33 official declaration PDFs. Version 2 contains 155 races and 1,404 declared runners, including 54 declared horses absent from the old result fields. These totals describe different cohorts; one is not a filtered runner count of the other.

All declared horses stay in the forecast. Later nonstarters receive zero winning target mass; they are not removed after the outcome to make the prediction easier. [1]

02 / Put time back in

Implemented boundary · incomplete historical proof

Old information
is not always earlier evidence.

A page about January, downloaded in November, does not prove what a forecaster could have read in January.

We separate the time of the event, the publisher’s date claim, and the actual observation or archive capture. Strict evidence requires a verified pre-cutoff observation or capture. Publisher-dated retrospective work instead accepts supported publication and update dates under an explicit availability assumption.

Date-only claims use the end of that date in Europe/Madrid. Unknown timezones are not invented. The current H1 declarations have publisher timing claims, not independent historical byte-availability certification. Fully strict historical runs remain blocked. [2]

The operational ambition is scheduled-start-minus-60-minutes. The experiments here use day-before deadlines. A scheduled off time, a verified actual off time and an observed price timestamp are three more distinct clocks; none can silently substitute for another.

What this taught usTemporal validity is a property of the evidence chain, not of the date printed in the story.

03 / Search, then admit

Implemented research architecture

Give the agent a question.
Keep control of the evidence.

The agent can decide what to look for. It cannot decide that a late page was available earlier, or turn a search snippet into a source.

A bounded search, not an omniscient one

Each race starts a fresh discovery session. Queries interleave horses, jockeys, trainers and stables. The original engine allows one to three rounds; defaults cap a race at 12 searches, 12 pages and 64 HTTP requests. Public article and social adapters record inaccessible or undated material as gaps.

The research agent selects real candidate IDs. Original pages are fetched and dated independently. At most eight paragraphs are admitted, and at most five exact sentence selections reach the forecaster within the context budget. Search snippets remain discovery-only. [3]

The roles are not independent experts

The default planner, researcher, judges and predictor share local llama3.1:8b weights. Separate prompts and conversations control data flow, but their errors can be correlated. Unanimity is an admission rule, not a probability that the evidence is safe.

The code and audit logs enforce a message boundary. They do not themselves provide operating-system isolation or eliminate memorized race outcomes. Source text is untrusted evidence, never an instruction to the agent.

The checkpoint that changed the question · 5 November

We tried 4-, 8- and 12-runner fields: six direct-LLM forecasts across paired closed-book and researched arms. Only three distributions were valid. Across 36 searches, no passage was admitted; many sources lacked supported dates. There was only one race with two valid arms. A lower score there could not demonstrate a research advantage: no new evidence had entered.

So we had two bottlenecks to separate: getting useful evidence through the pipeline, and producing reliable full-field probabilities. [4]

Why add event identity to temporal review?

A later preview can describe the target race as an earlier performance. A date elsewhere in the paragraph does not make that claim safe. An experimental event-grounded resolver therefore classified sentence-level event identity and cited code-owned time anchors before the three judges.

The corrected small real-passage replays admitted no useful cases and no leakage cases. Blocking everything is conservative but does not make a useful research system. This optional policy is an experiment, not a demonstrated solution. [5]

What this taught usMeasure each handoff. An empty evidence packet can be a retrieval failure, a dating failure or a review decision.

04 / Test a tiny world

Synthetic fixtures · not forecasting results

Before trusting a real race,
make a small one.

The main racing dataset is historical. The synthetic data is a deliberately controlled world for checking whether the plumbing behaves as specified.

How the synthetic fixture is assembled

The deterministic engine tests define one invented 1,200 m race at “Synthetic Venue,” with runners ALPHA, BETA and GAMMA, a January 10 target date and a January 9 cutoff. A fake source contains an earlier-form paragraph. Fake research and model adapters return prescribed plans, judgments, sentence selections and a distribution of 0.50 / 0.30 / 0.20.

The important part is that every input and expected decision is under our control. There is no fitted simulator generating a synthetic racing population, and this three-runner fixture is not a dataset for estimating predictive accuracy. [6]

Change one thing at a time

Move the source after the cutoff: admission must fail before judge calls. Add one judge veto: the text must never reach the predictor. Return an unknown sentence ID: the selector must not inject text. Supply duplicate runners, non-finite numbers or a bad total: the forecast stays invalid.

Separate local-model smoke checks exercise the positive path with synthetic evidence and the installed model. Four synthetic leakage probes test earlier history, an undated target outcome, prompt injection and a post-cutoff source. They verify selected behaviors, not population-level judge sensitivity.

What this taught usSynthetic examples make rules falsifiable. Real data is still needed to learn whether the rules are sufficient.

05 / Separate the jobs

Statistical baseline + feature experiments

Let language read.
Let the model estimate.

Instead of asking the language model to invent a complete probability vector, we gave it a smaller job: extract source-grounded features.

The statistical baseline already encodes six historical signals: smoothed win rate, appearance count, recent finish strength, distance-weighted win rate, days since last recorded appearance, and missing history. Unknown or nonfinishing positions use a fixed neutral strength of 0.5, an explicit modeling assumption.

Historical fitting uses strictly earlier calendar days, excluding same-day results. For the 2026 development predictions, histories freeze at the end of 2025. Fifty of the 368 declared January–February runners have no matched history; they retain explicit missingness.

Training has its own chronology

The baseline fits on 799 races from 2022–2024. Regularization is selected on 343 January–September 2025 races; the model then refits on those combined 1,142 races. Temperature is chosen on a separate 89 October–December 2025 races. The selected L2 penalty is 0.1 and temperature is 0.75.

Race-level cross entropy makes horses compete within each field. Temperature scaling changes distribution sharpness; it does not prove calibration. Neither regularization nor temperature is selected using a 2026 result. [7]

01 / BASELINE

Past recorded performance

A frozen statistical distribution from pre-2026 histories.

02 / FORM

Literal declaration strings

Order-independent summaries: known form, debutant status, reciprocal placings, win fraction and code indicators. Leading zeros are preserved; codes are not invented finishing positions.

03 / ARTICLES

Grounded recommendations

Optional source-backed features. An unavailable or failed extraction does not remove the horse or erase its deterministic form.

The probability target and fitting objective

Let D be the complete declared field and I(t) the information admitted under the chosen policy by cutoff t. The target is P(winner = i | D, I(t)), with i in D. It is a probability over the declaration available at prediction time, rather than a distribution retrospectively conditioned on which horses started. The benchmark retains later nonstarters with zero winning target mass.

The linear baseline minimizes mean race cross entropy plus an L2 penalty: −(1/R) ∑r log pr,winner + λ‖w‖². Standardization uses fitting data only. In the offset experiments, the baseline log probabilities stay fixed and only extra feature coefficients are learned. Softmax gives a coherent race distribution, but does not guarantee calibration, causal feature effects or historical validity of its inputs.

Article availability is itself a feature of the collection process. “Unknown” is not evidence against a horse. The observed correlation between recommendation-known and selection in January is why the later opposed-horse behavior needs a new, independently specified protocol.

What this taught usSeparating extraction from estimation lets us test language understanding and forecast quality independently.

06 / Fix the comparison

Recorded correction · 12 November

The experiment changed
two things at once.

At first, the agent-feature arm looked worse than deterministic form. But it had also been trained on fewer races.

The form arm used all 20 January races. Failed extraction removed four races from the agent arm, leaving 16. Even when we evaluated the same 17 successful February races, this was still a comparison of different training cohorts.

More decisively, every accepted agent form vector matched the deterministic parser, and all five semantic article columns were zero. A deterministic-only refit on the same 16 January races reproduced the old agent probabilities to numerical precision.

The correction

Keep deterministic form for every declared runner. Treat accepted article features as optional additions. Preserve failures as failures, but do not let them remove otherwise usable training races. Fit both feature arms on the same 20 January races and evaluate all 28 February races.

In this corrected zero-article-signal release, the two feature arms were effectively identical: maximum probability difference 8.42 × 10−8. The tiny numerical difference is not evidence of article value. The old result did not establish that useful agent information makes prediction worse. [8]

What this taught usA comparison can have correct scores and still answer the wrong causal question.

07 / Give text its own test

Controlled-source component experiment

A citation can be exact.
The interpretation can fail.

The next experiment isolated a single horse and a single question: is the publisher currently selecting it, opposing it, or saying neither?

The preserved corpus contains 29 complete paragraphs from three sources: earlier November context and previews concerning January 11 and February 1. Eligible passages must name the whole horse, match the target event date, and pass the publisher-documented availability check.

The local model chooses selected, opposed or unknown, plus a supplied evidence ID. Code copies the complete paragraph from that ID. This prevents invented quotations; it cannot establish that the paragraph supports the interpretation.

There is no outcome, price or probability in the extraction task. Labels enter only the later CPU evaluator. This is a controlled-source test, not an end-to-end evaluation of internet retrieval. [9]

Illustration / a deliberately invented passage

“ALPHA was our pick last month. For Saturday’s race, we prefer BETA.”

An exact quote names both horses. It does not make ALPHA a current selection. A grounded extractor must resolve who the recommendation concerns and when it applies.

What the experiment actually got wrong

On 26 selected reference cases, 20 were classified correctly (76.9%). The references were reviewed by the coding assistant before inference; they are not independent human annotations, and the selected set does not estimate population extraction precision.

Four current picks appearing only in a compact tip list were missed. One historical opponent was mistaken for a current selection; one withdrawn former favourite was labelled opposed instead of unknown. Correct source pointers did not prevent those semantic errors.

What the training set could not teach

January contains selected recommendations but no opposed recommendations. The opposed coefficient therefore remains zero. Selected and known flags are collinear in January, receiving equal positive coefficients.

As a result, an opposed horse in February can receive an uplift through the known flag. The model has not learned a credible opposition effect. Earlier sources covering both stances and independent extraction review are needed before promotion.

What this taught usEvaluate extraction coverage, semantic correctness and downstream prediction separately. Success in one does not imply success in the others.

Score the full field

For a race with one winner, yi is 1 for the winning horse and 0 otherwise. The multiclass Brier score sums squared errors over the whole field; reported results average those race scores equally. It is not a pooled binary score divided by the number of runners.

Brierr = ∑i ∈ field (pi − yi)²
Log lossr = −log pwinner

Log loss penalizes confident misses strongly. A zero-probability winner yields infinite loss. Forecast validity, forecast coverage and forecast quality must all be reported.

Keep the unit of uncertainty honest

Races at the same meeting share conditions. The paired analysis resamples whole meetings, preserving their races, with 4,000 bootstrap draws and seed 20261112. February has only six meeting clusters.

The intervals describe these fixed model predictions; they omit model-fitting uncertainty. Small-sample pooled-runner reliability plots and ten-bin ECE are descriptive. New feature arms have no separate fitted calibration and no per-horse probability intervals.

The article model improves the point Brier score by 0.00208 relative to form. Its descriptive ten-bin ECE is 0.01667 versus 0.01823 for form and 0.02663 for baseline. These are useful diagnostics for planning a new study, not sufficient grounds to claim reliable improvement or certified calibration.

One more distinction / disagreement versus skill

The market is a comparator with its own provenance.

For complete fields, the evaluator constructs a reported-price proxy by normalizing inverse prices:

qi = (1 / oddsi) / ∑j (1 / oddsj)

These values come from post-result reported fields under a decimal gross-return assumption. They are not certified closing or T−60 prices. Entire races with incomplete or invalid price fields are excluded from that comparison; missing horses are never assigned invented prices.

On the same 17 February races with complete proxies, article-model Brier is 0.822971, form is 0.827570 and the proxy is 0.799499. The proxy scores better on that subset. These numbers must not be compared with the 28-race table as though the populations were identical.

Verified exact closing fields and certified historical T−60 fields for this evaluation: zero. A gap pi − qi measures disagreement. It does not demonstrate that the model is right or that an executable bet has positive value.

What this taught us“Did the forecast improve?”, “Did it beat the market?” and “Does it disagree with the market?” are three different questions.

09 / The next experiment

Make the next result
harder to fool ourselves with.

01

Review the reading

Add independently reviewed examples, handle compact tip lists, and distinguish current recommendations from earlier opponents and withdrawals.

02

Broaden the evidence

Collect more earlier preview dates, both positive and adverse recommendations, and report coverage before measuring score changes.

03

Freeze the comparison

Fix extraction, missingness, cohorts, fitting and calibration choices before evaluating March–June. Keep the earlier failures and corrections visible.

04

Move toward a real deadline

Prospectively capture declarations, evidence and complete prices with timestamps. Commit predictions at the deadline, then score later outcomes.

The system is the chain:
what we knew → how we read it → what we predicted → how we checked.
